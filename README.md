# AspectStance

Data and induced aspect assignments for **AspectStance: Aspect-Oriented Stance Detection through
Target-Specific Fine-Grained Clustering**.

Milad Allahgholi and Hossein Rahmani, School of Computer Engineering, Iran University of Science
and Technology.

Stance detection usually assigns one label to a target treated as a single entity. Real targets are
discussed through many distinct facets, and a short post often addresses only one of them.
AspectStance induces a target-specific inventory of latent aspects without aspect supervision, and
conditions a stance classifier on the induced aspect.

---

## Pipeline

Per target, fitted on that target's training split only.

**Training**

1. **Enrichment.** Each tweet is paired with a concise, target-conditioned explanation generated by
   OpenAI `o4-mini`, which resolves implicit references and makes the referenced facet explicit.
2. **Encoding.** The original tweet and its enrichment are encoded separately with OpenAI
   `text-embedding-3-large` (3072 dimensions each).
3. **Reduction.** A single UMAP mapping is fitted per target and applied to both embeddings,
   reducing each to 20 dimensions. The two reduced vectors are concatenated into one
   40-dimensional representation, so both halves share a common reduced coordinate system.
4. **Normalisation and clustering.** Representations are L2-normalised, then clustered with K-Means
   under cosine similarity (spherical K-Means). Clustering is run for every candidate cluster count
   K = 2 … 30, and the solution with the highest Silhouette coefficient is retained. Its clusters
   are that target's aspect inventory.
5. **Naming.** Only after clustering is fixed, `o4-mini` reads the texts of each cluster and returns
   a short human-readable name. **This step does not create clusters, does not move any instance
   between clusters, and does not determine the number of clusters.** It is interpretation, not
   assignment.
6. **Classifier.** A per-target ensemble of K-Nearest Neighbors, XGBoost and Random Forest is
   trained on the 40-dimensional representation concatenated with a one-hot encoding of the aspect.
   Members are combined by soft voting: predicted class probabilities are averaged with equal
   weight and the highest-probability label is returned.

**Inference**

A new tweet follows the same enrichment and encoding path, is projected by the *frozen* UMAP
mapping, L2-normalised, and routed to the nearest *frozen* training centroid by cosine similarity,
which is the same criterion that formed the clusters. The stance classifier then predicts under the
routed aspect. Clustering never sees test data.

Normalisation belongs to the clustering and routing path. The stance classifier consumes the
unnormalised 40-dimensional representation at both training and inference.

## Results

Metric: **F1_avg**, the mean of the Favor and Against F1 scores, averaged with equal weight over the
targets of each dataset. This is the macro-average across targets (F-macro_T). It is *not* the
pooled F-micro_T that SemEval-2016 uses for its official ranking, so these numbers are not
interchangeable with official competition results.

| Method | P-Stance | SemEval-2016 Task 6 |
|---|---|---|
| **AspectStance** | **90.8** | **85.31** |
| Base Method (same ensemble, aspect feature removed) | 80.7 | 69.6 |
| Zero-shot prompting (`o4-mini`) | 81.4 | 72.4 |
| Retrieval-augmented few-shot prompting (`o4-mini`) | 83.2 | 74.9 |

The Base Method differs from AspectStance in exactly one feature block, so the difference of 10.1
and 15.71 points is attributable to aspect conditioning rather than to enrichment or dimensionality
reduction.

|---|---|---|---|

| P-Stance | Biden | 12 | 0.89 |
| P-Stance | Sanders | 9 | 0.93 |
| SemEval-2016 | Atheism | 5 | 0.94 |
| SemEval-2016 | Climate Change | 5 | 0.87 |
| SemEval-2016 | Feminist Movement | 5 | 0.85 |
| SemEval-2016 | Hillary Clinton | 6 | 0.89 |
| SemEval-2016 | Legalization of Abortion | 5 | 0.92 |

Silhouette is computed on the reduced 40-dimensional representation, which is the space the
clustering was performed in. UMAP tends to produce well-separated groups, so these values show that
the retained partition is a good partition *of that representation*; they are weaker evidence about
structure in the original embedding space, and they say nothing about whether the groups match
facets a human would recognise.

## Scope and limitations

Stated here so that the repository does not promise more than the study establishes.

- Each prediction is returned with the index of the aspect cluster it was conditioned on. The stance
  label remains a stance **toward the target**: neither benchmark provides facet-level annotation,
  so this is not a stance toward the named aspect, and it is not evidence of what the classifier
  relied on.
- Cluster names are generated after clustering and are not validated against human judgement.
- No hyperparameter search was performed. All components run at library defaults, and each test set
  was evaluated once. The reported figures are therefore what the pipeline yields untuned, not its
  ceiling.
- Results are from a single run. No variability across seeds, no confidence intervals, no
  significance testing. UMAP and the clustering are both stochastic.
- Only dataset-level aggregates were retained; per-target scores for the ablation are not available.
- On the SemEval-2016 targets, one cluster per target absorbs a large share of the data, so the
  decomposition is fine-grained for part of the data and coarse for the rest.
- Enrichment is model-generated and can introduce incorrect background, including confusing the
  target with a similarly named entity. Its accuracy was not measured.
- A separate model is trained per target, so labelled data is required for every target. The
  framework does not address cross-target or zero-shot settings.
